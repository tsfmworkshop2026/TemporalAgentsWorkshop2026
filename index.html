Overview

Why This Workshop

Time series data drives high-stakes decisions across finance, healthcare, climate and energy, manufacturing, scientific discovery, and IT operations. Yet the research landscape has become increasingly fragmented — forecasting vs. imputation vs. generation, univariate vs. multivariate and spatiotemporal, specialized inductive-bias models vs. foundation model approaches — producing incompatible assumptions, evaluation protocols, and tooling that slow progress toward deployable, reliable systems.

This workshop argues that the field is ready to move beyond isolated task papers toward time series foundation solutions: integrated systems that learn shared temporal representations, ingest heterogeneous context, support multiple query types, quantify uncertainty, satisfy physical or operational constraints, and remain robust under distribution drift.

A timely opportunity comes from the rapid emergence of agentic AI. Modern agents can plan, call tools, maintain memory, coordinate models, verify intermediate results, and operate in closed-loop workflows. For time series, this shifts the goal from answering a single forecasting query to building agentic temporal intelligence systems that decide what information to retrieve, which tools or simulators to invoke, how to adapt models to a domain, and when to defer, refine, or verify predictions.

This workshop therefore seeks to broaden the scope of time series foundation models from pretrained predictors to agent-ready temporal foundation systems — supporting flexible query interfaces, multimodal context grounding, memory over historical cases, tool use, physical and causal constraints, and trustworthy evaluation protocols.

Call for Papers

Goals & Topics

We welcome work across modeling, theory, datasets, systems, and applications — especially submissions that bridge multiple layers of the solutions stack.

Topic 01

From Task-Specific Prediction to Temporal Query Answering

How can a single foundation model support forecasting, imputation, anomaly detection, generation, counterfactual reasoning, and simulation through a unified query interface?

Topic 02

Agentic Workflows for Time Series Analysis

How should agents decompose temporal tasks into data preparation, model selection, uncertainty estimation, tool use, and result verification? How can multi-agent systems coordinate forecasting, diagnosis, planning, and monitoring?

Topic 03

Context, Memory, and Multimodality

How can temporal foundation models combine working memory, long-term memory, and retrieved domain knowledge from metadata, events, logs, text, images, and graphs while remaining robust to distribution shift?

Topic 04

Physics-Informed and Constraint-Aware Models

How can foundation models incorporate physical priors, differentiable simulators, state-space structure, and uncertainty propagation? How can agentic systems verify constraint satisfaction before acting?

Topic 05

Generative and Probabilistic Temporal World Models

How can diffusion, flow, autoregressive, and state-space generative models produce calibrated future trajectories, plausible counterfactuals, and simulation-ready samples for planning and decision making under uncertainty?

Topic 06

Evaluation Beyond Benchmark Accuracy

How should we design benchmarks to evaluate robustness under distribution shift, calibration, constraint satisfaction, tool-use correctness, reasoning quality, and human trustworthiness?

Topic 07

Systems, Deployment, and Human-in-the-Loop Reliability

How can we build efficient foundation models, continual adaptation pipelines, and agentic monitoring systems that remain auditable and controllable, operating under latency, privacy, and compute constraints?
